# Reels and Shorts, batch 1

> Stage 1 of the growth plan. Ten short videos, each built on one city fact the
> site has already verified (`docs/status.md`, "City traps worth remembering").
> Every video sends people to the one page that holds the full, sourced detail.
>
> Written 2026-09-13. **Re-check each fact on its linked page before you film.**
> If a page's `last_verified` has moved or the fact has changed, the page wins.

## How to use these

- **Format:** 20 to 35 seconds, vertical, text on screen, one voice-over. The
  same file works as an Instagram Reel and a YouTube Short.
- **Style:** same as the site (`docs/writing.md`). Plain English, no emojis, no
  em dashes, German terms kept and explained once.
- **Link:** Instagram captions cannot hold clickable links, so put the page in
  the bio link and say "link in bio". YouTube Shorts can take the full URL in
  the description.
- **Tracking:** use the tagged link in each script so Vercel Analytics shows
  which video brought people in. Replace `N` with the reel number.
  `?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=reel-N`
- **Posting:** schedule through Meta Business Suite (or Metricool once it is
  connected). Nothing goes out without the owner approving it.

## 1. You cannot register online (national, post this first)

**Hook on screen:** "Registering your address online in Germany? Not if you are from India."

1. Germany has an online address registration (Anmeldung) service.
2. It needs a German ID card, or an eID card that only EU and EEA citizens get.
3. A residence permit card does not work for it.
4. So if you are arriving from outside the EU for the first time, you register in person.

**Closing line:** "Which office, and whether you need an appointment, depends on your city. Link in bio."
**Link:** `/guide/anmeldung`

## 2. Saarbrücken: the permit office is in another town

**Hook:** "Studying in Saarbrücken? Your residence permit is not handled in Saarbrücken."

1. Residence permits for Saarbrücken are handled by the ZAB at the LAVA.
2. That office is in Lebach, about 30 km away.
3. Plan the trip before your first appointment.

**Closing line:** "The address and how to book are on our Saarbrücken page."
**Link:** `/cities/saarbrucken/residence-permit`

## 3. Cologne: nine offices, and only one is yours

**Hook:** "In Cologne, going to the wrong foreigners' office costs you weeks."

1. Cologne splits residence permits across 9 district offices.
2. Which one you use depends on your registered address.
3. Register your address first, then find your office.

**Closing line:** "Our Cologne page shows how to find the right one."
**Link:** `/cities/cologne/residence-permit`

## 4. Aachen: not the city

**Hook:** "RWTH students: your residence permit is not handled by the City of Aachen."

1. Residence permits in Aachen are handled by the StädteRegion Aachen.
2. That is the regional authority, not the city's own office.
3. Search for the right name, or you will land on the wrong page.

**Closing line:** "The right office and how to contact it: link in bio."
**Link:** `/cities/aachen/residence-permit`

## 5. Essen: closed on Wednesdays

**Hook:** "Do not plan your Essen permit visit for a Wednesday."

1. The Ausländerbehörde (foreigners' office) in Essen is closed on Wednesdays.
2. Check the opening hours before you take a day off for it.

**Closing line:** "Opening hours and booking for Essen are on our page."
**Link:** `/cities/essen/residence-permit`

## 6. Munich: book before you land

**Hook:** "Moving to Munich? You cannot walk in to register your address."

1. Munich's Bürgerbüros (citizens' offices) need an appointment for Anmeldung.
2. If you arrive without one, you will be sent away.
3. For residence permits, the office itself quotes waiting times of up to 10 months.

**Closing line:** "How to book both in Munich: link in bio."
**Link:** `/cities/munich/anmeldung`

## 7. Stuttgart: two tasks, one visit

**Hook:** "In Stuttgart you can do two big tasks in one visit."

1. At any Stuttgart Bürgerbüro except Mitte, you can register your address.
2. In the same visit, you can apply for your first residence permit.
3. Bring the documents for both.

**Closing line:** "What to bring is on our Stuttgart page."
**Link:** `/cities/stuttgart/anmeldung`

## 8. Leipzig: new slots at 17:00

**Hook:** "Cannot find an appointment in Leipzig? Check at 5 pm."

1. Leipzig releases new appointment slots on weekdays at 17:00.
2. You can also walk in to any of its 15 Bürgerbüros, whatever your address.
3. When you move within Leipzig, re-registering also updates the address on your residence permit.

**Closing line:** "Everything for Leipzig: link in bio."
**Link:** `/cities/leipzig/anmeldung`

## 9. Ulm and Magdeburg: you can still walk in

**Hook:** "Two German cities where you can still register without an appointment."

1. Ulm takes walk-ins for Anmeldung, Monday to Friday.
2. Magdeburg has a walk-in window from 08:00 to 09:00.
3. Go early. Walk-in queues fill up.

**Closing line:** "Your city might be different. Check it on our site."
**Links:** `/cities/ulm/anmeldung`, `/cities/magdeburg/anmeldung`

## 10. Munich tuition: same city, two answers

**Hook:** "Two universities in Munich. One charges non-EU students tuition. One does not."

1. Since winter semester 2024/25, TUM charges non-EU students tuition.
2. It is €2,000 to €3,000 per semester for a Bachelor, and €4,000 to €6,000 for a Master.
3. LMU, in the same city, charges no tuition.
4. In Bavaria, each university sets its own fee, so check yours.

**Closing line:** "Tuition for all 41 cities is on our site. Link in bio."
**Link:** `/cities/munich`
