### 1.3 Steuer-ID vs Steuernummer — difference

**Draft addition to `guides.intro_md`** (new short paragraph, since the current
guide doesn't explain this distinction at all — it's one of the most common
newcomer points of confusion):

> Don't confuse your Steuer-ID with your **Steuernummer**. The Steuer-ID (11
> digits, issued once by the federal BZSt) is permanent — it never changes,
> even if you move city or change your Finanzamt. The Steuernummer (10–13
> digits depending on the state) is issued by your *local* Finanzamt and is
> used specifically for filing tax returns and other Finanzamt correspondence
> — it **can change** if you move to a different Finanzamt's area. If you're
> filing a Steuererklärung for the first time, your Finanzamt will assign you
> a Steuernummer automatically; you don't need to apply for one separately.

- Confidence: **confirmed** — this Steuer-ID (federal, permanent) vs.
  Steuernummer (local Finanzamt, can change) distinction is consistent and
  well-documented. Source (representative, not government but consistent
  with BZSt's own framing of IdNr as permanent):
  https://www.bzst.de/DE/Privatpersonen/SteuerlicheIdentifikationsnummer/steuerlicheidentifikationsnummer_node.html
  — accessed 2026-07-05 (confirms Steuer-ID is BZSt-issued/permanent).
  Secondary corroboration via search synthesis, accessed 2026-07-05.

### 1.4 Tax classes (Steuerklasse I–VI) — what each means

**Draft addition, new `guides` content — could go in `intro_md` or a new
paragraph; Verifier/Builder to decide placement** (existing intro_md already has
one sentence on this; below expands it properly):

> Germany has six tax classes (Steuerklassen) that determine how much wage tax
> (Lohnsteuer) is withheld from your paycheck each month. They do **not**
> change your final tax bill — only the amount withheld upfront, which is
> reconciled against your Steuererklärung (or automatically, for many
> employees). The classes:
>
> - **Class I** — singles, divorced, or permanently separated people with no
>   children. This is the default for most newcomers.
> - **Class II** — single parents with at least one child registered in their
>   household; gives an extra tax-free allowance (Entlastungsbetrag für
>   Alleinerziehende).
> - **Class III** — one partner in a marriage/registered partnership, usually
>   the higher earner, when the couple chooses the III/V combination. Gets the
>   lowest withholding.
> - **Class IV** — both partners in a marriage/registered partnership with
>   similar incomes; each partner gets their own basic tax-free allowance.
>   (IV/IV is the automatic default for newly married couples unless you
>   apply for something else.)
> - **Class V** — the other half of the III/V pairing; goes to the lower
>   earner and has noticeably higher withholding (no basic allowance applied
>   here — it's balanced out at the III side).
> - **Class VI** — applied to a second or further job/employment. Highest
>   withholding, no allowances at all.
>
> For a married couple, IV/IV (or IV/IV with "Faktor") is simplest and roughly
> matches each partner's own tax liability, while III/V front-loads more net
> pay to the higher earner monthly but requires a Steuererklärung to true up
> — do the math for your specific incomes, or use one of the many free
> "Steuerklassenrechner" tools online, before choosing.

- Confidence: **confirmed** for the six-class structure and general
  who-gets-what rules. Source: search synthesis drawing on multiple German tax
  guidance sites, accessed 2026-07-05 (aggregator sources, not a single
  government page — the class definitions are legally grounded in §38b EStG).
  **Recommend Verifier confirm §38b Einkommensteuergesetz (EStG)** as the legal
  basis citation directly (paralleling the existing `legal_basis` field which
  cites §139b AO for the Steuer-ID) — we did not fetch the statute text
  directly this pass.
- The "IV/IV with Faktor" option (a fairer split for dual-income couples with
  different incomes, avoiding the need to owe money at year-end) is worth a

### 1.5 How to change tax class — the "genuinely useful part"

**Draft addition to `guides.documents_md` / `after_md`** (this is new — the
existing guide only says "tax-class-change form from your Finanzamt" without
saying how):

> To change your tax class, submit an **"Antrag auf Steuerklassenwechsel"**
> (application for tax class change) — this is now handled as part of the
> broader **"Antrag auf Lohnsteuer-Ermäßigung"** form family since a 2025
> ELSTER reorganisation. Two ways to do it:
>
> 1. **Online via ELSTER** (recommended, and since 1 October 2021 available
>    for married couples/registered partnerships changing between III/V and
>    IV/IV combinations): register for a free "Mein ELSTER" account at
>    elster.de, then find the form under Formulare & Leistungen → Alle
>    Formulare → Lohnsteuer Arbeitnehmer → Antrag auf Steuerklassenwechsel.
>    If both partners are changing class together (e.g. into or out of III/V),
>    **both must sign/submit** — except switching *from* III/V *to* IV/IV,
>    which only needs one partner's application.
> 2. **On paper at your local Finanzamt** — same form, submitted in person or
>    by post; useful if you don't want to set up ELSTER access.
>
> A tax class change generally only makes sense for married couples/registered
> partnerships (deciding between III/V and IV/IV) or when your family
> situation changes (e.g. you marry after arriving in Germany, or become a
> single parent). Singles cannot switch out of Class I. The change normally
> takes effect from the **start of the calendar month after** you submit the
> application — it is not retroactive within the same month.
>
> You can generally only change class **once per calendar year** without
> special justification (e.g. divorce, death of a spouse, or reconciliation
> after separation are exceptions that allow another change).

### 1.6 ELSTER and tax-return basics

**Draft addition, new short section (could be its own guide paragraph or part
of `after_md`):**

> **ELSTER** (elster.de) is Germany's free official portal for online tax
> filing and communicating with your Finanzamt — the name stands for
> "ELektronische STeuerERklärung." Register once (a "Mein ELSTER" account,
> free, requires ID verification by post or eID which takes about **1–2
> weeks** to activate) and you can file your annual Steuererklärung, request
> a tax-class change, and receive/respond to Finanzamt correspondence
> digitally.
>
> **Do you have to file a tax return?** If you're a normal employee with wage
> tax already withheld by one employer and no extra income, you are generally
> **not required** to file. But it is **almost always worth filing anyway**
> (a "freiwillige Steuererklärung" / Antragsveranlagung) — most employees get
> money back, because deductible costs (commuting, work equipment, moving
> costs for a job, etc.) usually exceed the flat allowance already assumed in
> your monthly withholding.
>
> You are, however, **required** to file if any of these apply: you received
> wage-replacement benefits (e.g. Elterngeld, Arbeitslosengeld) over a certain
> threshold, you had more than one employer at the same time, you're on the
> III/V tax-class combination as a couple, you have freelance/rental/other
> income over roughly €410/year, or the Finanzamt has directly asked you to.
>
> **Deadlines:** if you're required to file and do it yourself (no tax
> advisor), the standard deadline for a given tax year is **31 July of the
> following year** (so the 2025 tax year is due 31 July 2026); with a tax
> advisor or Lohnsteuerhilfeverein, the deadline extends to roughly the end
> of February the year after that. If you're filing **voluntarily** (not
> required), you have up to **4 years** after the end of the tax year to
> submit and still claim a refund.

- Confidence: **confirmed** for the mandatory-vs-voluntary distinction, the
  refund-likelihood framing, and the 31 July / extended-with-advisor deadline
  structure. Source (secondary, aggregating standard German tax-filing rules,
  consistent across multiple sources checked): search synthesis, accessed
  2026-07-05. **Flag for Verifier**: please confirm the exact 31 July 2026

### 1.7 Wrong tax class → refunds

**Draft addition to `guides.after_md`** (ties together 1.4/1.5, reassures
newcomers who default into Class I/VI unfairly at first):

> It's common for newcomers to be placed in Class I (or Class VI on a second
> job) by default, even if a different class would suit their situation —
> sometimes it also takes the Finanzamt/ELStAM system a few weeks to update
> your record once you do apply for a change, meaning your employer may keep
> withholding at the old rate for a pay cycle or two after you've filed the
> paperwork. **This is not a permanent loss.** Being in the "wrong" tax class
> never changes how much tax you actually owe for the year — it only affects
> your month-to-month cash flow. Any excess withheld is returned to you when
> you file your Steuererklärung (mandatory or voluntary, see above). If you
> believe the Finanzamt made an error in your final tax assessment
> (Steuerbescheid) itself, you can file an **Einspruch** (formal objection)
> within **one month** of receiving it.

- Confidence: **confirmed** for the core principle (tax class affects
  monthly withholding only, not final liability; refunded via
  Steuererklärung) and the one-month Einspruch window on a Steuerbescheid.
  Source: search synthesis of standard German tax-procedure guidance
  (§355 AO governs the one-month Einspruch period — not fetched directly this
  pass), accessed 2026-07-05. **Flag for Verifier**: confirm §355 AO citation

## Note on scope discipline (per hard rules)

No fake city variance was invented for anything in Part 1 (national how-to)
— Steuer-ID, Steuerklassen, ELSTER, and tax-return rules are stated as
uniformly federal, consistent with the honesty gate. Part 2 city variance is
limited strictly to **which office building/entity serves you and whether a
concrete address can honestly be given** — no invented fees, wait times, or
appointment requirements per city, since none of the sources found any
such variance (Finanzamt visits for tax-id purposes are overwhelmingly
mail/ELSTER-based, not in-person/appointment-based, unlike Anmeldung/ABH).
